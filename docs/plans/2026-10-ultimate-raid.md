# The Ultimate: Every Name

*2026-10-29. Design draft, nothing built. Names are working titles. The user chose the shape: **one villain, with each act standing for a set and the fallen bosses answering as echoes**. Sources: `docs/research/ff14-raid-boss-design.md` (Ultimates are long, phase-chained and memorised), the twelve fights in DESIGN.md section 4, and the FF taste in `set-3-pitches.md`.*

## Decided (the user, 2026-10-29)

- About **15 minutes**, one continuous pull (an exception to the 8–10 minute rule, for this fight only).
- **100% fixed.** Nothing shuffles per pull. It is learned, then performed.
- **One difficulty.** There is no Normal and Hard.
- **The Hollow King's call-and-response is the spine.** This fight is the sum of every raid, so it may borrow the court.
- Every earlier fight's key mechanic comes back **reworked, not copied**, and the fight shows the game's progression tastefully.
- The secret raid comes after this. Savage is out of scope.

## The villain

**The Cinder Sovereign**, the Ember the realm is named for. It is the fire under every room the party has fought in. Every boss the party killed burned back into it. When Caelum's meteor broke, the last of the star fell into the Ember too, and now it is awake.

The Ember never fights in a boss's body. It has its own body and its own moves, and it *calls* the fallen up out of itself.

**The look** (the user's pick, 2026-10-29: a mix of the Crowned Ember and the Kindler; concept at https://claude.ai/artifact/RjBb94YfGv4DU6PLR7be8J):
- **Body:** tall, still and regal, with a dark face and two ember eyes. A high collar, and a robe that burns away at the hem.
- **Smoke:** below the knees the robe becomes a column of smoke, so it glides and never walks.
- **Arms:** a thin molten blade held low in one hand and an ember cupped in the other. Four smoke arms are folded behind the two real ones.
- **The halo:** a ring of twelve sigils turns around it, each holding one boss's flame. When a name is called, that flame flies to the floor and becomes the echo's sigil, and its place on the halo stays dark. The party can count who is left, and in the finale the halo is nearly dark.
- **It evolves through the fight** (the user, 2026-10-29). Each act's set piece changes the body, easing in over a couple of seconds:
  - **Act I:** held back. A shorter blade, a small halo, a robe that only smoulders at the hem.
  - **Act II:** slag-iron plates grow over its shoulders and collar with glowing seams, the blade lengthens and runs hotter, and the smoke thickens.
  - **Act III:** the second form. The halo opens into the wheel of blades, the robe burns away to the knee to show a molten core, the eyes become a slit of white fire, and star-glints appear in the smoke.
  - **Act IV:** white-gold, with everything burning at full. The robe is in tatters, the blade at its longest and the core bare. It looks magnificent and close to burning itself out.
  - **The kill:** the fire drains and the core gutters out.
- **From above (the in-game read):** the halo turns at the edge of melee range, the blade points the way it faces, and the smoke trails behind. A scorched ring sits under it on the floor. The hitbox ring is bright at the front. The user wants the top-down view to be detailed and cool, since that is what the party sees. An echo is a ghost of the boss in that boss's own drawn style, but burnt: dark with an ember rim, half-formed, and gone the moment its answer lands.

Villain lines: one at the pull, one at the last act, none in between. It speaks the names of the fallen as cast names, not as dialogue.

## The story (told by the fight, not by talking)

The user asked for a story that isn't cringe. So nobody explains anything. The story is in where you are standing, who answers and how they go. It fits what is already canon (`docs/plans/story.md`, the set 3 notes): the kings built the gate so the road would lead *away* from the star's crater, and the words on the back of the gate were chipped away.

**The reveal: the town is named for what fell there.** When the star came down beyond the realm, one ember of it fell short, into the valley. The kings built the gate and the whole King's Road to lead everything *away* from that spot, and a town grew up over it and forgot. That is Emberfall. The sealed fourth portal in the square doesn't go somewhere else. It goes **down**.

**Walking the road back home.** Each act's room is a step back down the road toward town, drawn as the Ember remembers it, in cinders:
- **Act I:** the King's Road halls (kitchen tiles, silver threads, the beacon, the Barrow's braziers).
- **Act II:** the high places (the crater rim, the ice, the tower floor, the flooded temple).
- **Act III:** the star's crater under the meteor.
- **Act IV:** **Emberfall itself**: the square, the fountain, the portal ring, the bluebells, all burning in the Ember's memory. The party fights for the town on the town.

**What the echoes show.** Every hungry thing that moved into the road's halls was drawn there by the Ember. The bosses weren't random. A player works it out from the echoes, which rise from the Ember's fire and kneel to it before they answer. The only one who never kneels is the **Hollow King**, the king who wouldn't lie down. He was guarding the road all along. He answers the Sovereign's calls because he must. But in Act IV his lantern passes Doom *away* from the party, and the last thing he does is hand the lantern over.

**The two lines.** At the pull, the Sovereign says the town's name. At Act IV, it says the name of the first hero who fell this pull. If nobody has fallen, it says nothing.

**The ending.** When the Sovereign goes out, the echoes don't fade. They **lie down**, one by one in the order the party first met them, and the Hollow King is the last. A king who wouldn't lie down finally does. Then the burning square goes dark, and the party is standing in the real one at night.

**After the first clear:**
- The game gets its first epilogue page in the story book.
- Hask counts four back in.
- The words on the back of the gate can be read whole.
- The town's reward is never announced beforehand.

## The idea (the DESIGN.md questions)

1. **What is happening in this room?** Under the town, the ember that gave Emberfall its name calls up every boss it ever drew to the road, while the party walks the road back home through its memory. The last stand is on the town itself.
2. **The question the fight keeps asking:** "Who is answering, and what did they always want from us?"
3. **The rule a player learns:** **the Sovereign calls a name, and that boss answers with its rule a fixed beat later, from where its sigil burns.** Every echo does one thing: the one thing that boss was about (see the table). A player who remembers what a boss was *about* can read an echo they have never seen in this fight. Later acts call two names at once, and the rules combine.
4. **How it warns before it tests:**
   - The cast bar names the fallen ("Grubnak, the Ogre Chef").
   - At the same moment, that boss's sigil lights in the floor where the echo will rise.
   - The echo forms there over about 3 s, already holding the prop of its rule: the cleaver, the rune, the Sunwheel.
   - Only then does its telegraph appear, in full and in the usual colours.
   - The call is the warning, and the warning *is* the premise: a summons.
5. **Acts:** I **The Kindling** (set 1), II **The Forge** (set 2), a wall **The Heart Remembers**, III **The Fall** (set 3, second form), IV **Every Name** (the finale).
6. **How it ends:** the Sovereign calls all twelve. They answer in the order the party first met them, three at a time, one from each set, inside the Sovereign's own sword dance, while the last lantern passes as it did in the Long Night. The enrage is **Emberfall**.
7. **What Hard changes:** there is no Hard. The fight is the Hard of the whole game.
8. **Luck:** where a boss would pick someone, it picks at random, as it always did (Grubnak's soak marker is the model). Every pick is shown for the whole call. Which boss answers, and when, is fixed.
9. **The tank and the healer:**
   - The tank holds the Sovereign all fight. They take its own busters and aim the echoes that follow the tank (Vhalgrath's Talon Dive, Corvane's facing, Brannoc's fist).
   - The healer gets the game's heal checks as a progression: the Tithe in Act I, the Cry and the pools in Act II, Grace Withdrawn in Act III, and all three in reach of each other in Act IV, never two inside one window.
10. **What only this fight does:** it is a fight made of every other fight, and the party's whole history is the tell.
11. **What players will talk about afterwards:** "The Brannoc wheel during the Hrimgar floor" and "who had the lantern on the Caelum dance."

## How each boss answers (reworked, not copied)

An echo keeps its boss's **rule**, so the party can read it, but its **move is new**, with its own flair (the user, 2026-10-29: no exact copies). Each one is set alight by the Ember and does something that boss never did. A veteran recognises the question at once and still has to learn the answer.

| Echo | The rule it keeps | Its new move |
|---|---|---|
| Grubnak | where do I stand | **Flambé Service.** Four cloches come up, served in the order their rims fill, one hero to a plate. Each plate served lifts its lid and sends a line of flambé racing to the *next* plate in the order, so the order also draws the burning path between plates. Stand on yours, then step off the line that leaves it. |
| Skitterra | what do I break | **Burning Web.** Threads of fire run wall to wall and cut the room into pieces. Break a knot and its thread doesn't vanish: it burns out from the knot toward both walls like a fuse, so where you cut decides which way the fire runs. A thread left whole goes up all at once, with her shadow racing along it. |
| Volkar | who do I pair with | **Ember and Ash.** Every hero is fire or ash until the act ends. The charges *pull*: opposites drift toward each other and the same charges push apart, gently, all the time. A Surge lands on pairs. The floor is a little magnetic, and Act I's later moves are fought while drifting. |
| The Hollow King | what did I just see | **The Crier.** He doesn't swing. He rings his bell before every call in the act, and his two knights replay *the Sovereign's* last Ashen Sweep from the far side a beat later. His own answer is Royal Doom, cured by a lantern lit from a halo flame, so the party carries the Ember's own fire to save the doomed. |
| Vhalgrath | where will it land | **Cinder Dive.** He dives along a line of embers onto one of three scorched spots. Where the line crosses a sunk sheet the water boils, and a beat after the landing, steam columns burst up along every crossing. The landing rings out, then the steam. |
| Hrimgar | what will the floor do | **Glass Floor.** The Ember has turned his ice to red-hot glass. He stamps, and a crack **ripples** out from his foot sheet by sheet in a painted wave, each sheet shattering as the wave reaches it. You don't dodge a sheet, you outrun a wave or step through it once it has passed. |
| Azerath | which element, who holds the rune | **Twin Brands.** Two brands go on two non-tanks, and one of the four walls lights in an element. The element decides whether the two must **meet at that wall** or **flee from it to opposite corners**. Later in the act the lit wall changes once mid-cast, shown in full. |
| The Hydra | what you cut is remembered | **Heads of Flame.** Three heads of fire rise from floor sigils one at a time and bite at random heroes until cut. A cut head's stump stays alight as a small pool, and at the wall they erupt again **as pillars in the order you cut them**, with the Sovereign's blade sweeping after the last. |
| Corvane | what is his stance telling us | **Crossed Blades.** He kneels beside the Sovereign. His stance names the Sovereign's next unleashing, and then **both** perform it, his half mirrored: two Crescents crossing, two Lances from either side, a Pyre that cuts from both ends of the numbered order at once. |
| Brannoc | where will it bounce | **Sunwheel of Embers.** The wheel's whole bounce path is painted, with a blue catch point. Every bounce leaves a **wall of fire** for a few seconds along the leg it just ran, so the path becomes the room's walls. Catching it puts the fire out early. |
| Morrow-Oak | where do we let it eat | **Seeds of Ash.** A seed rides a hero, and where it bursts the floor **burns through to the town below**: a hole for the rest of the fight, glowing with the square's fire. A burning sapling climbs out of each new hole and walks toward the Sovereign. Kill it before it arrives, or it relights a dark sigil on the halo and that name is called again. |
| Caelum Vey | where will he be next | **Two Dancers.** His afterimages show where *the Sovereign* will be, numbered, and on the last pass Caelum himself steps into the line and cuts alongside it. Two blades and two lines fall on each beat, both shown before the first cut. The meteor is back in the sky above the act. |

### How the echoes grow up

The echoes themselves show the game's progression:
- **Act I:** an echo answers once and goes. One rule at a time, as set 1 taught it.
- **Act II:** an echo changes the room, and the change outlasts it (sunk sheets, scorched spots), as set 2's floors did.
- **Act III:** an echo *stays* at its sigil for the rest of the act and joins the Sovereign's moves, the way set 3's bosses danced.
- **Act IV:** the halo is dark, so the Sovereign calls the fallen up from the floor sigils they left behind. The Hollow King comes without being called.

### The dance pass (2026-10-29, from `docs/research/ff14-ultimates.md`)

The user wants the fight to feel like a dance the party learns: the boss can stand still, but the telegraphs steer the four of them through the room, and what looks random on the first pull turns out to be choreography. The real Ultimates do this with **a personal schedule per hero** (a number, a countdown ring) laid over **a shape that is the same every pull**. Each act gets one such block, about 25 to 45 s, in the place where it fits the story. They are not a rule for the whole fight, and nothing outside these blocks changes.

| Act | Block | Taken from | What the party does |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **Halo Procession** (the first Halo Turn grows into it) | Pantokrator | The four stand together. Two lit arcs of the halo sweep around the Sovereign as red wedges, and which way they turn shows in their first inch of movement, so the party walks a lap ahead of them. Each hero has a countdown ring, a few seconds apart; when a ring closes, that hero bursts a red circle, so they step out of the group just before and come back after. The lap ends on a Cupped Ember, which pulls them together again. |
| II | **The Anvil Count** | Program Loop | Each hero gets one to four pips. Blue towers rise on the forge sheets in pip order, each one placed away from the hero it belongs to, so every soak is a run across a floor that Hrimgar's water has already cut up. A soak by the wrong hero, or none, is a raidwide, not a wipe. The healer's number sits in the middle so the healer never has the longest run. |
| III | **Foretold** | Fate Projection | Caelum shows the future: four ghost heroes stand in the room, each one marked with what will happen to its hero (a red spread, a blue share, one ghost carries a seed). Each hero walks into their own ghost before the Sovereign's blades arrive. The seed bursts where its ghost stood, so the party sees ahead where Act IV's next hole will be. |
| IV | **The First Signal** | Diamond Dust, Heavensfall | In each pass the first answer to land sets the frame, and the other two answers of that pass land on the same frame, turned with it. The turn is fixed per pass (the fight is fixed), so a party that has learned it reads the first answer and knows where the next two go. |

The patterns came with timings for human players: gaps of about 6 to 9 s between personal beats, and only the next strike shown in full. Every block keeps the telegraph rules: red for damage, blue for a soak or a share, and no safe spot is ever painted. Ideas left out for now: per-act DPS-check casts (the closing loops already do that job), conga-line tethers, beat-based debuffs, and Rewind pins.

**Rules that keep it fair:**
- Never more than three rules live at once, and three only in Act IV.
- A chain shows its next strike clearly, not the whole chain.
- Pairs are the highlight, not the whole fight. Every act has quiet damage windows between calls.
- The tank and the healer are never idle for more than about 10 s.
- The Sovereign is never untargetable for more than about 20 s.

## Spectacle: transitions, VFX and music

There is only one difficulty, so this fight gets everything. It has the biggest set pieces in the game, effects nobody has seen yet and a score that changes with the story.

**The rules spectacle never breaks:**
- No transition covers a live telegraph.
- Every set piece runs in a window where nothing can hit the party, and the Sovereign is out of reach for at most about 20 s (the wall included).
- Screen flashes stay under the existing soft cap (`FLASH_CAP` in `main.js`).
- No text tells anyone what to do. Cast names and the two villain lines are the only words.
- There is no cut-in for your own Ultimate (the user removed it on purpose).
- The host drives each set piece as a scripted step on `f.course` and sends one event with a start time. Every client plays it the same way, and nothing in it decides who lives.
- The low-effects setting gets a lighter version of every set piece, never a skipped one.

**Everything happens in the fight, with no cutscenes** (the user, 2026-10-29). Players keep control the whole time, can move and can press buttons. The camera may pull back or shake, but it never takes over. Nothing fades to black and nothing is skippable, because nothing is a scene.

**Transitions (the user, 2026-10-29):**
- **The boss is invulnerable.** Each set piece that turns an act (2, 4 and 5 below) opens with the Sovereign **invulnerable for about 3 seconds**, so the turn lands on its own. Hits on it show *Immune*, Ultimates and the Limit Break can't be wasted into it (bots and the UI hold them), and damage over the HP gate is dropped, as at any push.
- **The clock stops.** The enrage clock and the course clock **pause** for the whole set piece and pick up where they left off. So does every running timer on the party: debuffs, Brand's burn, Doom and DoTs. Nothing ticks down while the room changes.
- **Cooldowns keep running,** so a transition is a short breather, not a reset.
- **The wall is not a set piece.** The Heart Remembers is a mechanic, so its clock keeps running.

### The set pieces

**1. The way down (the pull).** The party arrives standing on a copy of the square's flagstones, in a dark hall with four cold braziers. On the pull the stones crack and sink a step into glowing seams under the party's feet, and cinder glimpses of the King's Road, the high places and the crater drift up past the edges of the room as ash. The party is in control throughout. The halo lights one flame at a time, twelve beats, and on the twelfth the Sovereign says the town's name. Kindling follows.

**2. The road burns back (78%, into Act II).**
- The four braziers blow out *inward*, their flames pulled into the halo.
- Seams of light race across the floor and crack it into sheets.
- The hall's walls burn away like paper, and the high places stand around the room's edge as burning silhouettes: the crater rim, the glacier, the tower and the drowned temple.
- The camera takes one heavy shake as the floor settles.
- The Act I track ends on a bar. Four struck bells ring, one for each dark sigil, and Act II comes in on the fourth.

**3. The Heart Remembers (the wall at 50%).** This is the wall presentation that the earlier walls never got.
- The edges of the screen darken toward the stumps (a vignette under the telegraphs, never over them).
- The music drops to a heartbeat and a low drone.
- Each replayed head strike lands with a slam on the beat and a crack in the floor from its stump.
- At the last strike everything stops for one held breath.
- Then the seal **shatters like glass**. Shards streak outward low and fast, and a white-gold ring runs across the whole floor.

**4. Rebirth (into Act III).** This is the biggest moment in the game, about 12 s long.
- The Sovereign drives its blade into the floor. Every fire in the room, the sheet seams and the scorched spots, is drawn into it as spiralling ribbons.
- The room goes dark for a second and a half. Only the party and the halo are lit, and the music stops.
- **The halo opens.** It grows to the edge of the room and becomes a wheel of twelve blade-sigils. The dark ones stay dark, so the party can see who is left.
- The sky splits and the **meteor rekindles** overhead, the set 3 meteor come back. The floor becomes the crater.
- The boss bar gets its second-form look, a cinder rim on the stacked bars.
- A choir hit lands on the halo's opening, and Act III starts at full weight with no intro.

**5. Every Name (20%, into Act IV).** The walk home ends.
- The crater floor peels away in strips like burning paper. Under it is **Emberfall's square**: the fountain, the portal ring, the Tankard's sign and Osric's bluebells, all burning. The holes the party made in Act III burn straight through.
- **The camera pulls back** to show the whole square, wider than any fight has ever been framed.
- The Hollow King climbs out of the portal ring uncalled, lantern lit.
- The Sovereign says its second line (the name of the first hero who fell this pull, or nothing).
- Act IV's music brings in the town's own instruments, set on fire.

**6. The last flame (the kill).** There is no banner and no explosion.
- The Sovereign's ember gutters and goes out, and the halo's last flame drops.
- The echoes **lie down**, one at a time, in the order the party first met them. Each gets a soft bell, and the music loses one instrument with each.
- The Hollow King is last. He sets the lantern down on the stones and lies down beside it.
- The fire drains out of the square, and for a moment there is silence.
- Then the room cools around the party into the **real square at night**, lit only by windows. The party is still standing and free to walk, and the town tune plays on one whistle.
- The results card comes up after the tune's first phrase.

**7. Emberfall (the enrage).** The fire comes up *from under* the square: every flagstone seam goes white-gold, the town ignites from below, and the screen burns to ash from the edges in. It is not a white screen; the party watches it happen.

**A wipe** gets its own beat. The square's fire flares brighter and the halo relights in full, as if nothing was learned. Then the wipe card shows.

### Music

One theme carries the whole fight, the Sovereign's: a slow five-note figure that climbs and falls back a step. Each act plays it in the sound of its set, and the instruments pile up as the party walks home. All the tracks are in **D minor**, the key of the title screen (`mus-title`, "the last warm night before a long journey"), so the last fight of the game echoes the first thing a player hears.

| Track | Plays | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| `mus-ult-1` · The Kindling | Act I, about 2:30 loop | The title screen's lute and cello darkened, low whistle and frame drum, the theme stated plainly |
| `mus-ult-2` · The Forge | Act II, about 2:30 loop | Full orchestra, brass and anvil strikes, the theme heavy and driving |
| `mus-ult-heart` · The Heart | The wall, about 0:30 loop | A heartbeat, a low drone and one held string note, nothing else |
| `mus-ult-3` · The Fall | Act III, about 2:30 loop | A wordless choir, glass and bells, the meteor's roar under it, the theme vast |
| `mus-ult-4` · Every Name | Act IV, about 3:00 | Everything at once, plus the town's fiddle, whistle and dulcimer; the theme and the town's tune together, getting bigger with each pass |
| `mus-ult-end` · Night in the Square | The kill, about 1:00 | One whistle playing the town's tune, then the dulcimer, and nothing else |

**Small cues in code** (`sfx-synth`, so `tools/audio/coverage.js` covers them):
- A short sting in each fallen boss's own colours for every call, twelve in all. Grubnak's has a cleaver's ring and Volkar's a thunder crack.
- The four bells of set piece 2.
- The choir hit of Rebirth.
- The twelve soft bells of the kill.

**Notes for generating:**
- AI music tools can't copy a melody exactly. If your tool can take audio (cover or extend from an uploaded file), feed it `mus-title` for `mus-ult-1` and `mus-town` for `mus-ult-4` and `mus-ult-end`. Otherwise the shared key, tempo and instruments carry the link.
- The prompts go into `docs/art/music-now.md` when the build starts, in the same Style and Lyrics format.

## Unlocks, practice, rewards
